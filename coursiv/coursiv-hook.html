The eight hook patterns and when to use them

Curiosity gaps show the result and hold back the method. Contrarian hooks challenge common advice. Number hooks set expectations, mistake hooks name a familiar problem, and stories open at a turning point. Questions invite a self-check, proof hooks promise evidence, and how-to hooks lead into a tutorial.

Why hook generators beat staring at a blank line

Compare twenty angles on one idea, pick the ones that fit, and adapt them to the details only you can share. The explanation under each hook shows what makes its pattern work.

Email subject lines: the same patterns, half the length

Email uses a 50-character guide. Curiosity and number hooks often fit, while a story or a case study may work better as the first line of the email. The generator flags longer suggestions.

Frequently asked questions

What is a hook?

The first line a reader sees before deciding whether to keep reading.

Which format should I choose?

Choose where you plan to post. Email and YouTube have shorter length guides.

Should I post the hooks as written?

Rewrite the hook in your own voice and verify its claims first.
